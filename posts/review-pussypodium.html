akiaura's pussypodium (Slowed) Is the Solo Track That Anchors the Catalog Bottom Half

A review of the 2024 akiaura solo release sitting at 62 million streams — and a useful demonstration of what the catalog does outside the LONOWN collaborative axis.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

The akiaura catalog, viewed in stream-count order, has a structurally clear top: Sleepwalker (the 2023 LONOWN collaboration, 182 million streams in its original) and Sleepwalker - Slowed (106 million streams) sit substantially above everything else in the project. Below that line, the catalog spreads out across a long tail of collaborations and solo releases that mostly hover in the low-to-mid millions. The exception worth noting is pussypodium (Slowed), a 2024 solo release that has accumulated 62.3 million streams on its own — making it the project's third-largest individual track and, more usefully, the cleanest single-track demonstration of what the akiaura catalog does when the LONOWN collaborator is not in the room.

The Solo Register

Where the akiaura / LONOWN collaborations tend toward the more dissolved, atmosphere-forward end of the genre's vocabulary, akiaura's solo work has historically operated with more witch-house-derived hardness — louder noise, harder kicks, the kind of mix decisions that suggest the producer is still actively listening to the SoundCloud witch house scene from 2014–2018 rather than just gesturing at it. pussypodium (Slowed) sits cleanly inside this solo register. The track is, by genre conventions, harder than most of what the canonical apparatus has been issuing in the last twelve months; the kick sits forward in the mix, the noise floor is unapologetically present, and the patience that defines the LONOWN collaborations is partially traded for a more rhythmically committed forward motion.

This matters because the akiaura solo catalog is, in some real sense, the genre's harder edge — the corner where the witch-house DNA the producer came up on remains most audible, and where the contemporary scene's softer pad-forward consensus has the least influence. pussypodium (Slowed) is one of the cleaner demonstrations of that edge, executed with the production craft the catalog has accumulated since the foundational 2023 releases.

Why the Slowed Version Outperforms

One of the interesting things about the track's commercial trajectory is that the slowed version has substantially outperformed the original — a pattern that has become the scene's default but that, in this specific case, partially contradicts the track's own structural argument. The original pussypodium operates with a rhythmic forward motion that the slowed version necessarily diminishes; the slowing softens what was, by genre standards, an unusually committed dance-music arrangement.

What the slowed version gains, presumably, is the broader playlist compatibility that the parallel-version distribution model is designed to capture. The slowed cut fits more comfortably on the genre's atmosphere-forward curations; it sits more easily next to Sleepwalker - Slowed and the broader mid-tempo angelcore canon than the original would. The audience that arrives at the track through algorithmic recommendation is, in most cases, looking for something that operates at the canonical scene's working tempo, and the slowed version provides that fit. The original is the more interesting record; the slowed version is the one the audience actually listens to.

Where It Sits

pussypodium (Slowed) is not the akiaura catalog's foundational document, and it is not the track most listeners encountering the project for the first time would land on first. What it is, structurally, is the cleanest mid-catalog demonstration of what the project does outside its most-cited collaborations — a useful entry point for listeners who arrive at akiaura through Sleepwalker and want to understand what the rest of the working catalog actually sounds like. Listen to the original first if you want to hear what the producer was trying to do; listen to the slowed version if you want to understand how the scene's audience actually consumes the catalog.
