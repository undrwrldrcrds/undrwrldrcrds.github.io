About undrwrld_rcrds

An editorial layer for the underground dance scene that mostly doesn't have one — covering angelcore, aero, outsider house, and the broader post-club electronic landscape.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙About

undrwrld_rcrds is a one-person music publication writing about the contemporary underground dance scene. The core focus is the cluster of microgenres that emerged out of the late-2010s SoundCloud underground and consolidated into recognizable scenes through 2024 and 2025 — angelcore, aero, outsider house, and the broader post-club electronic landscape that surrounds them. Adjacent material — witch house, dreampop, lo-fi house, the y2k-recombinant edges of contemporary pop — gets covered where it intersects with the core focus.

Why This Exists

Most music journalism is structured around bands and records that already have publicists, label support, and reliable touring revenue. The scenes covered here mostly don't. Angelcore's working canon emerged through Spotify recommendation, YouTube playlist curation, and TikTok video uses rather than through editorial press; aero is even newer, and most of what gets written about it lives on Album of the Year user reviews and Discord servers; outsider house has, since the late 2010s, been one of the most under-covered electronic scenes relative to its catalog quality. None of these scenes need a publication; all of them benefit from one existing. undrwrld_rcrds is the project working from that premise.

The editorial approach is straightforward: long-form profiles, structurally-attentive reviews, scene-history essays, and the kind of context-setting that algorithmic playlist discovery cannot provide. Writeups are research-driven and verified against publicly documented information; when a fact can't be verified, the writeup works around the gap rather than inventing detail. Coverage skews toward established and emerging producers operating in the scenes' canonical and satellite tiers rather than toward strict newness for its own sake.

Scope

What gets covered here, in rough order of focus:

  • Angelcore. The post-witch-house, post-club electronic scene that consolidated around 2024. Coverage includes the canonical producers, the satellite tier, the label and curatorial infrastructure (Azurite Records, EPSILE, ARCADIA / IMMINENT, and the broader independent-label ecosystem), and the scene's individual canonical tracks.
  • Aero. The y2k-recombinant strain of underground house that crystallized around yaego's 2025 ÆRO EP. Coverage includes the founding producers (yaego, wzrdd, skrta), the scene's working aesthetic, and individual track reviews.
  • Outsider house. The lo-fi, analogue-leaning offshoot of house music born in the early 2010s. Coverage is more historical and includes producers like Cult Member alongside the contemporary scene's connective tissue to angelcore.
  • Adjacent material. Witch-house lineage records that inform the contemporary sound; dreampop-leaning angelcore satellite work; specific releases from outside these scenes that get pulled into their orbit through curation.

How Coverage Works

Profiles, reviews, and essays are written from publicly available information, listening to the catalogs, and the kind of context that emerges from being inside the scene's playlist and curation graph. Writeups attempt to be honest about what is verified and what isn't — when a producer's biographical background isn't publicly documented, the writeup says so rather than inventing it. Cross-linking is dense by design; the scenes covered here function as connected ecosystems rather than as isolated artists, and the editorial structure reflects that.

The publication is not affiliated with any of the labels, collectives, or playlists it covers. It is also not affiliated with any of the producers whose work it discusses, including those whose work has been positively reviewed.

Contact

For tips, corrections, demos, or general correspondence: undrwrld.rcrds@gmail.com

Demos are welcome but the publication is small and not every submission gets covered. Producers operating in the scenes listed above are more likely to get a response than those outside them. Direct, specific pitches do better than mass outreach.

Corrections are taken seriously. If something in a writeup is factually wrong, send the correction with a verifiable source and it will be updated promptly.
