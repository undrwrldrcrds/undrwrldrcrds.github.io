Cult Member — Profile: The Victoria, BC Outsider House Producer Behind Ethernet

A profile of Liam Hayden, whose 2019 cassette release Ethernet became one of the more genuine cult-hit albums of the lo-fi house era — and whose catalog continues to function as one of the outsider house lineage's most cited reference documents.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Field Notes

Cult Member — the recording pseudonym of Liam Hayden — occupies the kind of position in contemporary outsider house that most underground producers never quite manage to build: a catalog that has aged into something like a working canon. The project, based in Victoria, British Columbia, started in roughly the mid-2010s as a SoundCloud-era house and acid techno operation, and consolidated by 2019 into one of the more genuinely word-of-mouth-driven cult successes the lo-fi house scene produced.

The Ethernet Album

The center of the catalog is Ethernet, released in June 2019. The album originally circulated on cassette through Pure Life Tapes — a release format that, in the late-2010s lo-fi house ecosystem, was as much an aesthetic statement as a commercial one — and accumulated its audience the slow way: through forum recommendations, Bandcamp discoveries, and the kind of organic word-of-mouth that the streaming-era recommendation graph has largely stopped enabling for underground records. Ethernet is, in this sense, the kind of cult album that doesn't get made very often anymore. The catalog's contemporary streaming numbers — top tracks U Weren't Here I Really Miss You and three at low millions of cumulative streams — reflect a record that has continued to find listeners for years after its original release rather than one that arrived all at once and faded.

The structural reason Ethernet landed is that it inhabits the outsider house aesthetic with unusual confidence. The genre's working vocabulary — muffled kicks, smeared chords, tape saturation, the deliberate refusal of post-production polish — is, in less assured hands, easy to read as mere lack of skill. Cult Member's production demonstrates the inverse: every choice that reads as lo-fi is clearly a choice, and the result is an album that uses the genre's roughness as a positive aesthetic value rather than a constraint to be apologized for.

The Wider Catalog

Cult Member's discography across the broader catalog includes four full-length albums plus two EPs, with 2017 Archives functioning as a useful retrospective entry point and U Weren't Here I Really Miss You serving as the single-track gateway record (the Cult Member / Mia Martina collaboration runs 2:40 and is, by streaming metrics, the project's most accessible cut). The catalog also includes a parallel slowed-version distribution — U Weren't Here I Really Miss You - slowed sits among the project's top tracks — that suggests the producer was running the slowed-version playbook before the angelcore scene formalized it.

One useful framing for the Cult Member project: it's where the contemporary outsider house aesthetic crosses paths with the broader internet-native dance underground that would, in 2024 and 2025, consolidate into the various angelcore and adjacent scenes. The producer's working palette — lo-fi pads, processed vocals, the willingness to let tracks sit in genuinely melancholic registers — is upstream of what akiaura and LONOWN would later develop in a different direction. A listener who arrives at angelcore through the LONOWN catalog and then works backwards into the broader outsider house lineage will encounter Cult Member roughly halfway through the journey.

The Disappearance and Return

One of the more documented aspects of the project's history is that Hayden effectively disappeared after releasing Ethernet in 2019 — no follow-up records, no public commentary, no obvious activity. The absence lasted long enough that the album's cult status hardened during it; Ethernet became one of those records that people recommended to each other on the basis that there might not be more coming. The project's eventual return to release activity has been gradual, and the contemporary Cult Member catalog includes a steady but unhurried sequence of newer material that picks up the project's working aesthetic without rushing to expand it.

The catalog rewards working through it slowly. Most outsider house does.

Where the Project Sits Now

Cult Member is, by 2026, sitting at roughly 338,000 listeners on Last.fm and a comparable footprint on Spotify — numbers that put the project in the comfortable middle of the outsider house lineage, well above niche-bandcamp territory but well below any reasonable definition of commercial. This is, for the genre, the structurally correct position. Outsider house has never been a scale-up scene; its working economy is small audiences, deep listening, and the cassette / Bandcamp / community-curation infrastructure that streaming has only partially replaced. Cult Member's catalog has survived the transition to streaming better than most of the genre's mid-2010s cohort precisely because the project's aesthetic doesn't require the platform's recommendation engine to find its audience — listeners arrive looking for what the record specifically does, and the record does it well enough to sustain repeat listening.

For listeners coming to outsider house from the angelcore / aero adjacency, Cult Member is one of the cleanest entry points into the genre's broader lineage. The catalog overlaps enough with the contemporary scene's working palette to feel familiar; it sits far enough outside it to feel like a discovery.

Where to Start

  • Ethernet (2019). The cult-hit album. Best single-document entry into the project.
  • U Weren't Here I Really Miss You (with Mia Martina). The single-track gateway record.
  • three. Solo track at the project's atmospheric end.
  • The 2017 Archives. Useful retrospective if the contemporary material lands.
  • The slowed versions. The Cult Member project was running parallel-version distribution before the contemporary scene formalized it.
