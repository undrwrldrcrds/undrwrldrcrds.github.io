The Sleepwalker Phenomenon: How a Witch-House EP Track Built an Entire Microgenre

An essay on how a 35-million-stream, 600-million-TikTok-view track came out of the SoundCloud underground in 2023 and accidentally laid the foundation for everything contemporary angelcore has become.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Scene History

The most useful thing to understand about the angelcore scene of 2026 is that it is, in some real sense, a scene built around a single track that nobody expected to do what it did. Sleepwalker — the akiaura / LONOWN collaboration that arrived in early 2023 as the centerpiece of the EP Ketamine Girl — was, at the moment of its release, a record fully consistent with the witch-house-derived corner of the SoundCloud underground its producers came up in. There was no commercial expectation. There were no editorial gatekeepers. The two producers were, at most, a few thousand monthly listeners between them. What happened next is the part of the story that has, three years later, hardened into the genre's working operating manual.

The First Six Months

The track moved on TikTok first. Not in the way most viral electronic tracks of the early 2020s had moved — through a single creator's use of a hook in a specific kind of video — but in a more diffuse pattern: small uses across multiple visual contexts, mostly melancholic, mostly soft-focus, mostly the kind of post-club affect that the witch-house lineage had been cultivating for a decade without ever quite landing. By mid-2023 the track had crossed a few million Spotify streams without any of the platform-level editorial placement that typically precedes that kind of growth. The track was, in effect, organically reaching the listeners the witch-house scene had been trying to find for ten years.

What unlocked the next order of magnitude was the slowed version. Released in parallel with the original — partly as a fan-driven response, partly as deliberate distribution by the producers and their adjacent communities — the slowed version of Sleepwalker became a parallel viral track in its own right, accumulating millions of its own streams without cannibalizing the original. The combined trajectory, across original and slowed and eventually ultra-slowed and sped-up versions, has by 2026 settled at something like 288 million Spotify streams, 800,000+ Shazam tags, and 600 million+ TikTok video uses. The track has crossed onto the Billboard Hot Dance chart and has sustained presence on the Spotify Viral 50 Global and Shazam Worldwide Electronic charts in a way no other angelcore-coded record has yet matched.

What the Genre Inherited

Most of what makes contemporary angelcore recognizable as a sound came directly out of Sleepwalker's working method. Five inherited moves, specifically:

  • The pitched vocal sample as primary hook. A short fragment, looped, processed past the point of literal legibility, doing the structural work a chorus would do in a more conventional pop record while refusing to be a chorus.
  • The witch-house low end welded to a softer upper register. The bass and the noise come from one tradition; the pads and the sample manipulation come from another. The combination is the genre's working timbre.
  • House tempo without house function. Tracks operate in the 120–135 BPM range that house music defined, but the kick is rarely the loudest element and the track is never built to fill a floor.
  • Structural refusal where structural release is expected. The drop never arrives. The chorus never lands. The song dissolves before it can resolve.
  • The parallel-version distribution model. Original, slowed, ultra-slowed, sped-up — each version released alongside the others, each finding its own audience, the working catalog effectively multiplying without the producer needing to write new tracks.

Each of these moves predates Sleepwalker in isolation. Witch house had been doing the pitched vocal samples since 2010. Slowed-version culture had existed in the SoundCloud cloud-rap economy since the mid-2010s. The structural-refusal move is, in some sense, as old as the L.I.E.S. outsider house catalog. What Sleepwalker did was bundle these moves together at a scale that made the synthesis legible — and then survive at that scale long enough that other producers had the time to reverse-engineer the working method.

The Subsequent Catalog

The akiaura / LONOWN follow-ups — Destruction Age (2024), Fake Everything (2025) — are, by some measure, the producers consolidating around what Sleepwalker already did. Destruction Age is the album where the angelcore aesthetic crystallizes as a coherent project rather than as a single-track accident; Fake Everything is the project working at full confidence, with the vocabulary established and the producers free to range across it. But the foundation, structurally and commercially, is Sleepwalker. Every subsequent angelcore record has been in conversation with it, whether by direct emulation or by deliberate deviation.

The producers most clearly in the akiaura / LONOWN lineage — DJ Anemia, crier, sixnite, Baby Jane, DJ Pointless, riserayss, deadwinter, and the broader ARCADIA / IMMINENT collective — have, between them, formalized the Sleepwalker-derived approach into a working canon. The ANGELCORE PLAYLIST mixtape series, the parallel-version distribution model, the curated YouTube mixes that function as scene infrastructure — all of these are downstream of the trajectory Sleepwalker opened.

What This Means for the Genre

The interesting and somewhat ambivalent thing about a scene built around a single founding track is that the scene's medium-term health depends on its ability to outgrow that track without abandoning the moves the track introduced. There are precedents both ways. Witch house never produced a track at Sleepwalker's scale and faded as a coherent scene within four years of its peak. Hyperpop produced a similar single-track foundation moment (the early SOPHIE catalog, then 100 gecs) and managed to outgrow it into a broader sound. Where angelcore lands on that spectrum is, by 2026, still an open question.

What is not in question is what Sleepwalker made possible. The track is, in the most direct sense, the reason the genre has the infrastructure it has — the ARCADIA / IMMINENT release apparatus, the curated mixtape ecosystem, the parallel-version distribution model, the playlist graph that lets a new producer get read into the scene within weeks of releasing their first record. All of this is downstream of three years and 35 million streams ago, when two producers from the witch-house corner of the SoundCloud underground released an EP that almost nobody was expecting to listen to.
