Destruction Age: A Close Reading of the Foundational Angelcore Album

On the akiaura / LONOWN album that took the moves Sleepwalker introduced and demonstrated they could sustain an album-length project — and that has, in the two years since, become the scene's most-cited single-document reference.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Close Reading

Destruction Age arrived in June 2024 as the second full-length collaboration between akiaura and LONOWN, following the 2023 EP Ketamine Girl. Where Ketamine Girl introduced the producers' working partnership as a recognizable project — and where the single Sleepwalker demonstrated that the underlying sound could move at scale — Destruction Age is the record that asked whether the partnership could sustain an album-length argument. The answer, two years later, has settled: Destruction Age is now the scene's most-cited single-document reference and the closest thing contemporary angelcore has to a canonical album.

The Inheritance from Ketamine Girl

To read Destruction Age usefully, it helps to first locate what it inherited from Ketamine Girl and what it deliberately moved past. The 2023 EP was still recognizably a witch-house project — the noise floors were denser, the percussion programming was more aggressive, the influence of the producers' SoundCloud-era backgrounds (the corecore lineage, the broader noise-and-ambient ecosystem that surrounded artists like Crystal Castles and Pictureplane) was more audible. Sleepwalker, the EP's commercial center, was as much a witch-house record as it was anything else; the angelcore label hadn't yet been applied to it, and the track would have fit comfortably on a 2014 Tri Angle Records compilation without much editing.

The shift from Ketamine Girl to Destruction Age is, in the most direct sense, the producers committing to the angelcore label and moving the sound to fit it. The pads soften. The percussion recedes. The noise floor stays present but moves further into the background. The upper register opens up. The witch-house DNA is still there in the low end and in the more aggressive moments, but the album's center of gravity has shifted toward atmosphere, toward pad-forward composition, toward the kind of structural patience that Sleepwalker only partially demonstrated.

The Album's Structural Logic

Destruction Age is, on close listening, doing structural work that the singles do not. The album's sequencing alternates between dissolved, atmosphere-forward stretches and more rhythmically intact tracks — a pattern that has since become the standard arrangement strategy for angelcore album-length projects. The dissolved stretches do the genre's emotional withholding work; the rhythmic tracks do the structural anchoring work; the alternation gives the album a forward momentum it would not have if it committed entirely to one mode.

The album's longest stretches are also its quietest. There are moments on Destruction Age that operate closer to ambient music than to anything you'd reasonably call dance — extended pad sections, field recordings drifting in and out, percussion either absent or so heavily processed it functions more as texture than as rhythm. These moments are doing what angelcore, as a genre, has not yet figured out how to do at single length: sustaining a single emotional register across enough runtime that the listener is forced to live inside the mood rather than skim past it. The album-length format is, in this sense, doing structural work the genre's broader streaming-economy format cannot.

The album operates closer to a mood piece than to a song cycle. The individual tracks are coordinates inside a single sustained register.

The akiaura / LONOWN Division of Labor

One of the things Destruction Age makes audible, more cleanly than the singles do, is the working division of labor between akiaura and LONOWN. Listened to in sequence, the album reveals roughly two compositional modes: a more aggressive, witch-house-coded mode that reads as akiaura-forward, and a softer, more melodically patient mode that reads as LONOWN-forward. Most tracks operate as some negotiation between the two. The album's most successful tracks are the ones where the negotiation is most balanced — the producers' contributions reinforcing rather than competing.

This is, by some measure, the album's most useful demonstration. The collaborations across the broader scene — DJ Anemia and crier, yaego and wzrdd, the various ARCADIA / IMMINENT-coordinated releases — all operate on similar working principles, but Destruction Age is the longest single document that lets a listener hear those principles play out across a project rather than a track. Subsequent angelcore producers working in collaboration have, whether they say so or not, been working from the template Destruction Age established.

What the Album Did to the Scene

The genre's editorial and curatorial apparatus largely consolidated in the year following Destruction Age's release. ANGELCORE PLAYLIST vol. 1, the DJ Anemia / crier mixtape that has functioned as the scene's foundational curatorial document, arrived in April 2025 — roughly ten months after Destruction Age. The IMMINENT label apparatus, which has anchored the parallel-version distribution model the scene now relies on, ramped up its release cadence over the same period. The broader ARCADIA-aligned playlist graph took recognizable shape across 2024 and into 2025.

None of this was caused by Destruction Age in a strict sense, but all of it became possible in a way it had not been before. The album functioned as proof-of-concept: the scene had a record. With a record, the scene could organize itself around something. The curatorial work, the parallel-distribution apparatus, the editorial conventions — all of these are downstream of the moment Destruction Age demonstrated that angelcore could sustain album-length attention.

Where to Start

If you have not yet heard Destruction Age, the recommended approach is to listen to the album in sequence, in a single sitting, without skipping. The album's structural argument depends on the alternation between modes; jumping to individual tracks via a streaming platform's algorithmic shuffle defeats most of what the project is trying to do.

If a single-track entry point is necessary, the album's title track is the most direct one. Sleepwalker, while on Ketamine Girl rather than Destruction Age, remains the gateway record for the entire akiaura / LONOWN catalog and is a reasonable starting point if no version of the album is at hand.
