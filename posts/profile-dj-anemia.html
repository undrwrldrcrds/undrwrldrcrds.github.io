DJ Anemia — Profile: The Producer-Curator at the Center of the Angelcore Editorial Layer

The producer and curator whose ANGELCORE PLAYLIST mixtape series, alongside crier, has functioned as one of the scene's most consistent editorial documents — and whose own track archangel has become a canonical reference point.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Field Notes

DJ Anemia occupies a slightly unusual position in the contemporary angelcore scene: the project operates simultaneously as producer and as curator, and the curatorial side has, by some measures, been as consequential to the genre's development as the production side. The ANGELCORE PLAYLIST mixtape series — produced primarily as collaborations with crier — has functioned, since the April 2025 release of ANGELCORE PLAYLIST vol. 1, as one of the scene's most consistent editorial documents outside the broader canonical apparatus release calendar.

The ANGELCORE PLAYLIST Series

The mixtape series is, in effect, the closest thing the genre has to a curated canon. Each volume operates as both a DJ set and a release in its own right — distributed on YouTube, sequenced as a continuous mix, and assembled with the kind of taxonomic care that the scene's broader playlists don't quite manage. ANGELCORE PLAYLIST vol. 1 opened with the akiaura / LONOWN / DJ Pointless collaboration If You Care, included archangel (the DJ Anemia / crier / sixnite collaboration that has become a canonical track in its own right), and folded in slowed-version recontextualizations of tracks from adjacent scenes — Snow Strippers' Under Your Spell, among others — that pulled the broader pop-adjacent context into the angelcore frame.

The subsequent volumes — vol. 2 (May 2025), and the rolling sequence of themed mixtapes that followed — have continued the same approach: roughly half original or first-released material, roughly half curated recontextualization, all sequenced as a continuous listening experience. The series has become the most reliable single document for understanding what the working canon of angelcore looks like at any given month.

archangel as Canonical Track

archangel, released December 8, 2024 on IMMINENT as a DJ Anemia / crier / sixnite collaboration, is the production-side track most central to the project. The track has been issued in original, slowed, and ultra-slowed versions (the ultra-slowed version released March 28, 2025 on ARCADIA), each accumulating significant independent streaming presence. archangel sits at the more vocally-forward, melodically-legible end of the genre — the sixnite vocal contribution gives the track an emotional center that some of the more dissolved angelcore productions deliberately resist — and the track has functioned as a useful gateway record for listeners coming to the scene from adjacent pop and alt-pop spaces.

The track's distribution pattern is also worth noting: the parallel release of original / slowed / ultra-slowed versions is the model that Sleepwalker first demonstrated and that archangel has now confirmed as standard practice for the scene's canonical tracks. Each version finds its own audience; the producer's working catalog effectively triples in size without the producer needing to write three separate tracks.

The Curatorial Method

One useful framing for the DJ Anemia project: it's the working example of how a scene with no traditional editorial layer — no Pitchfork-equivalent, no Resident Advisor-equivalent, no functioning music-blog ecosystem — generates its own canon. The curated mixtapes do the work that editorial criticism does in larger genres: they propose which tracks are central, which are adjacent, which are worth slowing and recontextualizing, which adjacent scenes are in conversation with the core project. DJ Anemia is, functionally, the scene's most consistent critic-by-other-means.

Whether this remains the case as the scene scales is an open question. The current arrangement — where curatorial authority and production authority live in the same project — is unusual for a music scene at this size. Most genres develop a separation between producers and editorial figures fairly early. Angelcore hasn't yet, and the DJ Anemia project is the central reason it hasn't needed to.

Where to Start

  • ANGELCORE PLAYLIST vol. 1 (April 2025). The foundational mixtape; the closest thing the scene has to a canonical curated document.
  • archangel (with crier and sixnite). The most direct production-side entry point.
  • archangel (Slowed). The most-streamed of the parallel-release versions; useful as a demonstration of how the genre's distribution model works.
  • The broader DJ Anemia & crier mix series. Each subsequent volume of the ANGELCORE PLAYLIST has functioned as a working canon for the month of its release.
