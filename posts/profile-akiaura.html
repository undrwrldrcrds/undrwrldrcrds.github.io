akiaura — Profile: The Sleepwalker Phenomenon and the Architect of Modern Angelcore

A working profile of the Saint Petersburg producer whose 2023 single Sleepwalker reframed what an underground electronic release could become — and whose collaborative discography with LONOWN forms the genre's canonical core.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Field Notes

akiaura is the producer the contemporary angelcore scene has had to organize itself around. By mid-2026 the project has crossed 3.3 million monthly Spotify listeners, with the foundational track Sleepwalker sitting at over 182 million streams in its original release and another 106 million on the slowed version. Born December 6, 1997, and based in Saint Petersburg, akiaura came up through the post-witch-house / corecore SoundCloud underground; the early discography leans on darker, more abrasive internet-native electronic music — closer in temperament to Crystal Castles or the broader witch-house lineage than to anything that would today be called house. By 2026, the project sits at the center of one of the most documented underground-to-streaming trajectories of the decade.

Sleepwalker and the Ketamine Girl Era

The 2023 EP Ketamine Girl — a collaborative project with frequent collaborator LONOWN — introduced akiaura to a wider audience. The EP's centerpiece, Sleepwalker, became one of the more anomalous viral tracks of the SoundCloud-to-Spotify era: across its original release, slowed versions, ultra-slowed versions, and sped-up edits, the track has accumulated something on the order of 288 million Spotify streams, 800,000+ Shazam tags, and 600 million+ TikTok video uses.

The trajectory matters for two reasons. First, Sleepwalker demonstrated that a track operating well outside any commercial dance music convention — pitched vocals, witch-house-derived noise, structural patience, the whole post-club register — could move at scale through the platform economy without any of the usual mediation by labels, radio, or editorial press. Second, the way the track moved (multiple slowed and remixed versions accumulating separately, each finding its own audience) became the template for how the genre's subsequent canonical tracks would be distributed. The slowed-version-as-parallel-release model that defines the ARCADIA / IMMINENT release graph is essentially the Sleepwalker model, formalized.

The LONOWN Collaboration

akiaura's working relationship with LONOWN is the genre's most consequential creative partnership. Beyond Ketamine Girl (2023), the pair released the full-length Destruction Age in 2024 and the follow-up Fake Everything in 2025 — with a Fake Everything (Slowed) edition arriving in 2026. Taken together, the three records map the genre's consolidation in real time. Ketamine Girl is the project as a still-witch-house-leaning EP; Destruction Age is the album where the angelcore aesthetic crystallizes; Fake Everything is the project working at full confidence, with the genre's vocabulary established and the producers free to range across it.

Beyond the album-length collaborations, the akiaura / LONOWN axis has produced individual tracks that have become genre reference points — If You Care (with DJ Pointless), Deathwish (with Baby Jane), and the foundational Sleepwalker itself. Most discussions of contemporary angelcore that don't start with one of these tracks are starting later than they should.

The Solo Catalog

akiaura's solo work runs in parallel to the LONOWN collaborations and is, on balance, the harder-edged side of the project. The solo discography retains more of the witch-house DNA the producer came up on — denser noise floors, more aggressive percussion programming, a willingness to let tracks sit in genuinely uncomfortable registers — and tracks like the solo entries on the One Wish compilation (2022) and various single releases function as a useful counterweight to the more atmospheric LONOWN collaborations.

The current commercial heft of the project — sustained Billboard Hot Dance and Shazam Worldwide Electronic chart presence, regular Spotify Viral 50 Global appearances — comes from the combined catalog rather than from any single record. Sleepwalker is the gateway track; everything else is the catalog people stay for.

What the Aesthetic Inherited from Witch House

One useful framing for akiaura's project: it's the producer who most completely metabolized the lessons of mid-2010s witch house — Crystal Castles, Pictureplane, Salem, the broader noise-and-pad ecosystem — and applied them to a house-tempo, post-club register. The supersaws and ambient pads of contemporary angelcore are doing the same emotional work that distorted samples did in witch house; the difference is mostly tempo and the explicit borrowing of dance music's architecture. akiaura's particular trick is that he kept the witch-house low end and grit while building the upper register out of the softer angelcore palette. The result is a sound that reads as angelcore from above and witch house from below.

Where to Start

  • Sleepwalker (2023, with LONOWN). The track that built the audience. Original version first; the slowed versions are useful afterward.
  • Ketamine Girl (2023, with LONOWN). The EP that introduced the project as a coherent statement.
  • Destruction Age (2024, with LONOWN). The canonical angelcore album. Required listening for anyone trying to understand the scene.
  • Fake Everything (2025, with LONOWN). The project at confidence; useful for understanding where the genre is moving.
  • Deathwish (with LONOWN and Baby Jane). A vocally-forward entry that demonstrates the akiaura / LONOWN production approach with a more legible song structure on top.
