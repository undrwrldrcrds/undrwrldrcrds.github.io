DJ Anemia, crier & sixnite's "archangel" Is the Curatorial Project as Production

A review of the December 2024 IMMINENT release that has become one of the angelcore scene's most reliable reference tracks — and a demonstration of how the genre's curatorial figures operate when they sit down to produce.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

archangel is the track that most directly demonstrates how DJ Anemia's broader curatorial project — the ANGELCORE PLAYLIST mixtape series, the YouTube-circulated mixes assembled with crier and adjacent collaborators — translates into actual production work. Released December 8, 2024 on IMMINENT as a DJ Anemia / crier / sixnite collaboration, the track has, in the year and a half since, become one of the angelcore scene's most reliable canonical reference points.

The production sits at the vocally-forward end of the genre spectrum. The sixnite vocal contribution is processed lightly enough to remain legible as a performance rather than dissolving into texture; the harmonic content is unusually melodic for the scene, with chord movement that does recognizable verse-and-chorus work rather than looping a single mood for the track's duration. This is, in effect, the curatorial sensibility translated into production: the same instinct that lets DJ Anemia sequence a mix to land emotionally on a casual listener also lets the producer write a song that lands emotionally on a casual listener.

The distribution model is also worth noting. archangel has been issued in original (December 2024, IMMINENT), slowed (early 2025), and ultra-slowed (March 28, 2025, ARCADIA) versions, each accumulating significant independent streaming presence. This is the Sleepwalker-derived distribution pattern operating at full confidence — three parallel releases of the same track, each finding its own audience, the producer's working catalog effectively tripling without the producer needing to write three separate tracks. The slowed version, in particular, has become the most-streamed of the three, which is consistent with the broader pattern across the genre.

The track is the working demonstration that angelcore can be popular without compromising its core aesthetic moves.

What archangel gets right, structurally, is the balance between the genre's atmospheric tendencies and the demands of a vocally-forward song. The track resists the temptation to fully dissolve the vocal into texture (which would have placed it closer to the genre's atmospheric extreme) but also resists the temptation to clean up the production into something pop-radio-ready (which would have placed it outside the genre entirely). The compromise — vocally legible, atmospherically dense, deliberately quiet — is the position the scene has been working toward, and archangel is the track that most clearly inhabits it.

Listeners coming to angelcore through the broader Spotify recommendation graph often arrive at archangel as one of the first scene-canonical tracks they hear. This is appropriate. If Sleepwalker is the genre's foundational text and Destruction Age is its album-length statement, archangel is the track that demonstrates the genre at its most welcoming.
