LONOWN — Profile: The Compositional Anchor of Modern Angelcore

Little is publicly known about LONOWN. What is documented is the work — and the work, at 6.7 million monthly Spotify listeners and three collaborative albums with akiaura, is the most consequential body of underground electronic music produced in the last three years.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Field Notes

LONOWN is one of the more deliberately opaque figures in contemporary underground electronic music. The producer maintains a minimal public presence — no detailed biographical interviews, no documented hometown, no birth year on the open record — and the project's social-media footprint is mostly the music and its visual context. What is documented is the discography, the collaborations, and the scale: by 2025, the project had reached over 6.7 million monthly Spotify listeners, placing LONOWN at the commercial top end of the entire angelcore / hardwave / phonk-adjacent ecosystem.

This profile is, accordingly, a profile of the work.

The Phonk-to-Angelcore Trajectory

One of the more interesting things about LONOWN's catalog — and one of the things that helps explain how the project arrived at its current commercial heft — is that the producer didn't start in angelcore. The early discography sits much closer to the phonk / hardwave / wave ecosystem that dominated underground electronic streaming through 2020–2023. Tracks like the early ZX3 (2017), the 2019 Heaven Subculture EP, and various 2019–2020 single releases (Turquoise Crystals, Fatality, the Virtuoso and Fallen Temper collaborations with Sace) operate in a register closer to drift phonk and hardwave than to anything you'd today call angelcore.

This is the trajectory the genre's recent grokipedia entry describes accurately: "many contemporary angelcore producers, such as akiaura and LONOWN, previously created music in the wave and phonk genres before transitioning to the more celestial and atmospheric production style of angelcore." The transition was gradual and is still visibly in progress across the catalog — the recent LONOWN solo work retains more of the phonk DNA in its low end and percussion programming than akiaura's solo work does — but the consolidation around the angelcore aesthetic, anchored by the collaborations with akiaura, is the project's current center of gravity.

The akiaura Collaboration

The akiaura / LONOWN partnership is the most consequential creative axis in contemporary underground electronic music. Three full-length collaborations to date — Ketamine Girl (2023), Destruction Age (2024), and Fake Everything (2025) — plus the genre-defining single Sleepwalker, which across its original and remixed versions has crossed 288 million Spotify streams and 600 million+ TikTok video uses. The collaboration's working pattern, audible across the three records, is roughly that LONOWN handles the compositional and chord work while akiaura contributes the noise, the textural grit, and the more aggressive percussion choices. Listen to the two producers' solo work side by side and the division becomes legible: akiaura's solo tracks are harder and more witch-house-coded; LONOWN's solo tracks are more melodically forward and structurally patient.

Beyond the album-length collaborations, the pair appear together on most of the canonical contemporary angelcore tracks — If You Care (with DJ Pointless), Deathwish (with Baby Jane), the various Anemia/crier-aligned compilation appearances. The collaborations are documented; the working relationship is otherwise private.

Solo Work and the riserayss Axis

LONOWN's solo output, separate from the akiaura collaborations, is anchored most prominently by the LONOWN / riserayss collaboration starly — a track that has functioned as the opening cut on multiple angelcore mixtapes and that, across its original and slowed versions, is one of the genre's most-streamed solo-attributed tracks. The newer LONOWN / riserayss material — Scorpion (2026) and adjacent releases — extends the partnership.

The solo catalog is otherwise diffuse: a long tail of 2019–2024 releases that document the phonk-to-angelcore transition, plus a steady stream of 2025–2026 single releases that operate fully within the contemporary angelcore register. The producer publishes at a high cadence; the catalog rewards working through it chronologically more than working through it by stream count.

What the Scale Means

6.7 million monthly Spotify listeners is, for context, an unusual number for a producer working in a niche this specific. It places LONOWN well above any reasonable definition of "underground" and into the territory of producers who can sustain a project full-time from streaming revenue alone. The number is also somewhat misleading as a measure of the scene's actual size — most of LONOWN's monthly listeners arrive via algorithmic placement on broader phonk and electronic playlists rather than via the angelcore-specific curatorial apparatus — but it does mean that the angelcore scene has, in LONOWN, a producer with the kind of commercial footing that most underground microgenres never produce. This matters for the genre's medium-term trajectory in ways that are worth watching.

Where to Start

  • Destruction Age (2024, with akiaura). The canonical angelcore album. The single most important record in the genre.
  • starly (with riserayss). The solo-attributed work that functions as the genre's most reliable opening track.
  • Sleepwalker (2023, with akiaura). The track that built the audience.
  • Fake Everything (2025, with akiaura). The collaboration at full confidence.
  • The 2019 Heaven Subculture EP. For listeners curious about the phonk-era origins; useful as a contrast with the contemporary work.
