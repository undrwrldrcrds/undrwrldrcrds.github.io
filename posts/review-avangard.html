LONOWN's AVANGARD (Slowed) Is the Solo Track That Quietly Outran Sleepwalker

A review of the 2024 LONOWN solo release that, at 107 million Spotify streams, has become the largest individual track in the project's catalog — and one of the contemporary angelcore scene's quietest commercial outliers.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

For most of the contemporary angelcore scene's first three years, the conversation about the genre's commercial top end has been organized around the akiaura / LONOWN collaborative axis — specifically, around Sleepwalker, the 2023 track whose combined stream count across original and slowed versions sits at roughly 288 million. Sleepwalker is, by every conventional measure, the genre's flagship commercial document. It is also, somewhat surprisingly, not the largest individual track in either producer's solo catalog. That title belongs to AVANGARD (Slowed), a 2024 LONOWN solo release that has, with relatively little discussion in the scene's editorial layer, accumulated 107 million streams on its own.

What the Track Is

AVANGARD (Slowed) is the parallel-distribution slowed version of AVANGARD, released through the broader ARCADIA / IMMINENT release apparatus in 2024. The original sits at substantial streaming numbers in its own right; the slowed version is the one that landed at scale. The track operates in the genre's most recognizable working register — patient chord work, witch-house-derived low end, deliberately quiet percussion — but with a slightly more melodic upper register than most of LONOWN's collaborative output with akiaura.

The track also represents one of the cleanest demonstrations of how LONOWN's solo work differs from the collaborative catalog. Destruction Age is loud — the noise floor on most of those tracks is audible as part of the composition rather than a side effect of it. AVANGARD is the opposite. The track is quieter than almost anything in the LONOWN solo catalog, and the production palette pulls noticeably more from late-night ambient than from the witch house lineage that informs most of the collaborative work. The result is a track that reads as both unambiguously canonical and noticeably easier to listen to passively than most of the genre's foundational records — which is, structurally, one of the cleaner explanations for why it has accumulated the streaming numbers it has.

Why the Number Matters

107 million streams is, in the contemporary angelcore scene's working context, an unusual amount of commercial heft for a solo track without an obvious editorial hook. Sleepwalker had viral TikTok traction; archangel had the IMMINENT release apparatus pushing it; worry had the scene's full curatorial network behind it. AVANGARD (Slowed) has, by visible metrics, accumulated its audience the slow way — through algorithmic recommendation, playlist placement on the broader phonk and electronic curations that LONOWN's solo catalog reaches into, and the kind of repeat-listen accumulation that builds up without anyone in the scene's editorial layer announcing it.

This is, structurally, what makes the track an interesting case. The contemporary angelcore scene has spent most of its working attention on the canonical tier — the akiaura collaborations, the DJ Anemia / crier curated mixtapes, the foundational tracks that get cited in scene primers. AVANGARD (Slowed) demonstrates that the scene's actual commercial activity is broader than that conversation captures. There are LONOWN solo tracks accumulating tens of millions of streams that most listeners following the scene's editorial layer have not specifically encountered, because the scene's editorial layer is organized around scene-internal canonicity rather than around aggregate streaming impact.

The track is the case for what the genre actually sounds like when nobody is curating it. The audience picks the easier, more melodic record. They have always done this.

The Mid-Tempo Center of Gravity

One of the things AVANGARD (Slowed) makes audible, more cleanly than most of the genre's collaborative records, is the existence of a mid-tempo melodic center inside the broader angelcore sound that the scene's canonical-tier records partially obscure. The witch-house-derived hardness of Sleepwalker, the dissolved atmospheric stretches of Destruction Age, the patience of worry — these are the records the scene's editorial conversation tends to organize around, and they are also records that demand a certain amount of listening attention from the audience to do their work. AVANGARD (Slowed) demands considerably less. The track is, in the most direct sense, easier to put on and let run.

This is not a critique. The genre's working aesthetic accommodates both modes, and the LONOWN catalog has always operated across the full range. What the track demonstrates is that the easier-listening end of the genre's range has substantially more commercial weight than the canonical-tier editorial conversation suggests. Most listeners arriving at angelcore through Spotify recommendation are encountering AVANGARD (Slowed) and tracks operating in its register, not Destruction Age's longer atmospheric stretches. The canonical apparatus and the algorithmic apparatus are reaching different audiences inside the same genre.

Why This Isn't Discussed More

The interesting structural question, given the track's commercial scale, is why AVANGARD (Slowed) has not generated more discussion in the scene's editorial layer. The most likely explanation is that the scene's working canon was set before the track's full streaming trajectory was visible — by the time the numbers crossed the 50 million mark, the canonical conversation was already organized around the akiaura collaborations and the foundational mixtape sequences, and the track ended up filed under "LONOWN solo catalog" rather than under "scene-defining release."

The other likely factor is that AVANGARD (Slowed) does not announce itself the way the canonical-tier records do. The track does not have an obvious editorial hook beyond its own production; it does not anchor a mixtape sequence; it does not have the kind of biographical or scene-internal context that makes a record easy to write about. It is, by its own structural choices, a track that lets itself be consumed passively. The streaming numbers reflect that; the editorial attention reflects the inverse of it.

Where It Sits

Listened to in 2026, AVANGARD (Slowed) remains one of the more useful single-track demonstrations of what LONOWN's solo project does when the collaborator is not in the room. The track is melodically forward, deliberately quiet, and operating at the easier end of the genre's range without compromising the production craft that the broader catalog is built on. It is also, by most working measures, the largest commercial document the contemporary angelcore scene has produced — which is worth knowing about, even when the scene's editorial conversation has organized itself around other priorities.
