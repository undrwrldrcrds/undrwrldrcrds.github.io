yaego's "HARDX" Turns a Justin Timberlake Sample Into the Aero Manifesto

A review of the ÆRO EP's lead single — a 3:15 demonstration of how aero metabolizes y2k pop into the post-club register.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

HARDX is the most ear-catching track on yaego's 2025 EP ÆRO, and it is the cleanest single-track demonstration of what aero is doing structurally. Released as the EP's lead single on May 9, 2025, ahead of the full project's July 10 release on the small label high off seahorse records, the track runs 3:15 — long enough to develop, short enough to fit comfortably within the genre's streaming-economy attention budget.

The track's organizing move is the sample. HARDX builds itself around a slowed-and-processed Justin Timberlake Rock Your Body echo — pulled forward into something recognizable enough that a listener who grew up with the original will catch it within a few bars, but processed thoroughly enough that the sample is doing emotional and textural work rather than functioning as a quotation. This is the move aero is built on: y2k source material, contemporary execution, the past and present collapsed into a single recombinant texture.

Around the sample, the production runs the rest of the aero playbook. Hypnotic supersaw synths in the upper register; shaking bass that owes its programming to 2014-era EDM more than to anything actually from 2003; percussion built to drive but mixed restrained enough that the track never quite arrives at a stadium-EDM drop. The first half of the track — what the reviewer at Album of the Year called "the intoxicating first part" — lasts roughly 90 seconds before transitioning into a heavier second half. The transition is abrupt by design; aero is, in general, more comfortable with structural discontinuity than its EDM and trance ancestors would have tolerated.

The track is what happens when a producer who grew up watching 5 Gum commercials sits down to make dance music in 2025.

The track also demonstrates aero's relationship to time. HARDX is not nostalgic about the y2k era in any straightforward way; it doesn't try to reproduce the sound of 2003 club music. What it does, instead, is treat the y2k pop archive as raw material — sampling, processing, and recontextualizing it through a contemporary production framework in a way that makes the source material strange. A listener who recognizes the Timberlake sample hears two records at once: the 2003 record the sample came from and the 2025 record the sample now lives inside. The pleasure of the track is in the gap between the two.

If aerotheworld is the EP's argument and musica electronica is its opening statement, HARDX is the EP's hook — the single most listenable, most immediate, most-quotable track in the project. It is, by some measure, the gateway record for the entire genre.
