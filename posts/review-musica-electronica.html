yaego's "musica electronica" Opens the ÆRO EP with a Statement of Method

A review of the opener of yaego's 2025 EP — three minutes of recombinant pop archaeology that establish the genre's working method before the project has fully announced itself.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

musica electronica is the opening track of yaego's 2025 EP ÆRO, and it is doing the kind of work opening tracks on five-song EPs are supposed to do — establishing the project's working method efficiently enough that the listener arrives at the second track already knowing what kind of record they are inside of. The track runs three minutes flat, which is the median length on the EP, and which is also long enough to develop a structural argument without overstaying it.

The track's organizing sample is from LMFAO's Shots-era catalog — specifically, the "shake that" tag that anyone old enough to have watched 2011 club music will recognize within a few bars. The choice is, on its face, almost a joke: LMFAO is, in most contemporary critical frames, exactly the kind of mid-tier 2011 EDM-pop that any serious electronic producer of 2025 should be ironic about. musica electronica handles the sample without irony, which is the move the whole aero project is built on. The y2k-through-mid-2010s pop archive is not the joke; the joke is whatever cultural framework taught a listener to think it was.

Around the sample, the track does the structural work the rest of the EP will then accelerate. The opening sets up a recognizable dance-music architecture — kick on the downbeat, hi-hats in the upper register, supersaw pads filling the harmonic space — then introduces a dembow rhythm into the lower half of the kit, which pulls the track sideways into a register that is neither EDM nor reggaeton nor house, but which uses the rhythmic vocabulary of all three. The structural payoff arrives in the form of a section that, per the AOTY reviewer's description, ends with "sounds of explosions without much in the way of cooldown" — a structural choice that, on a more conventional EDM record, would be a failure of arrangement, and that on musica electronica is the entire point.

The track refuses to release the energy it builds, then ends. The refusal is the song.

This is, structurally, the aero working method. Tracks build to a point that, in their source materials, would call for a release; the release is then withheld, or substituted with discontinuity, or simply allowed to end. The listener is left holding the energy the track generated. This is the move that distinguishes aero from straightforward EDM revivalism, and it is the move musica electronica demonstrates most efficiently.

If the EP has a thesis statement, musica electronica is one of the candidates for it. The track's recombinant sample work, its structural discontinuity, and its three-minute runtime together do most of the explaining the project needs to do — by the time the listener arrives at HARDX, the rules have been set.
