akiaura & LONOWN's "Sleepwalker" Is the Record the Scene Had to Build Itself Around

A retrospective on the 2023 single that, across its original and slowed versions, became the most consequential single underground electronic release of the decade — and the track every subsequent angelcore record has been in conversation with.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

Three years on, Sleepwalker is the kind of record that has stopped being a record and become a genre's founding gesture. Released in 2023 as part of the akiaura / LONOWN collaborative EP Ketamine Girl, the track has accumulated something on the order of 288 million Spotify streams across its various versions, 800,000+ Shazam tags, and 600 million+ TikTok video uses — numbers that put it well outside any reasonable definition of "underground" while remaining a track that any commercial dance-music gatekeeper of 2019 would have refused to release.

The composition itself is, in retrospect, restrained. A pitched vocal sample loops in the middle distance; a witch-house-derived low-end carries the track's weight; the kick is loud enough to read as dance music but soft enough never to drive. The hook is functionally one or two phrases, repeated past the point where conventional pop structure would tolerate. None of these are formal innovations in 2023; the witch-house lineage had been doing most of these moves since 2011. What Sleepwalker got right was the synthesis — the precise ratio of noise to pad, of dissolution to clarity, of repetition to development, that subsequent producers have spent three years trying to reproduce.

The track refuses to deliver the catharsis its first ninety seconds promise. The refusal is the song.

The slowed versions matter as much as the original. The Sleepwalker (Slowed) edition, distributed in parallel with the original, accumulated its own independent streaming audience and functioned as a kind of permission slip: the scene's subsequent canonical tracks would also be released across parallel original / slowed / ultra-slowed versions, each version finding its own listeners. The distribution model angelcore now relies on came directly out of how Sleepwalker moved.

The track's commercial trajectory — the Billboard Hot Dance chart presence, the Spotify Viral 50, the sustained Shazam Worldwide Electronic placement — is the part that gets discussed most often. The part that matters more, three years later, is what it did to the producers around it. Every contemporary angelcore record is in conversation with Sleepwalker whether the producer says so or not. The genre's working vocabulary — the pitched samples, the witch-house low end, the structural refusal, the slowed-version-as-parallel-release distribution model — is essentially the vocabulary Sleepwalker introduced.

Listened to in 2026, the track reads as both familiar and slightly anachronistic. Familiar because three years of subsequent angelcore has acclimated the listener to its moves; anachronistic because the witch-house DNA is more present in Sleepwalker than in most of what the scene is making now. The genre has, by some measure, moved away from the track's noisier edges and toward the softer, more pad-forward register of Destruction Age and the later LONOWN solo material. Sleepwalker sits, accordingly, as the record that opened the door — and as a useful reference point for understanding what the door opened onto.
