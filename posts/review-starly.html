LONOWN & riserayss's "starly" Is the Angelcore Opening Move

A review of the LONOWN / riserayss collaboration that has functioned as the opening cut on the genre's foundational mixtapes — and that demonstrates LONOWN's compositional approach at its most patient.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

starly is the track that, more than any other single record, demonstrates what LONOWN brings to the angelcore project as distinct from akiaura. Where the akiaura solo work and the akiaura / LONOWN collaborations lean on witch-house DNA — denser noise floors, more aggressive percussion programming, an audible willingness to let tracks sit uncomfortable — starly is unhurried, melodically forward, and structurally patient in a way the broader scene's canon often isn't.

The track's placement matters. starly has appeared as the opening cut on ANGELCORE PLAYLIST vol. 1 (April 2025) and across the subsequent DJ Anemia / crier-curated mixtape volumes; it has also opened the parallel prodvnxious and witxoutessence-tagged YouTube angelcore mixes that have populated the scene's broader editorial layer. Functionally, the track is the genre's most common opening move — the record that gets played when a DJ wants to establish what kind of mix the listener has arrived in.

The composition is restrained. The riserayss vocal contribution sits forward in the mix, processed lightly enough to remain legible as a vocal performance rather than dissolving into texture (a choice that distinguishes starly from a lot of the genre's more atmosphere-forward records). The harmonic structure builds slowly across what would, in a more conventional pop record, be the verse. By the time the track arrives at anything resembling a chorus, the listener has been inside it long enough to have stopped expecting one — which is, of course, the move the genre has internalized from Sleepwalker.

The slowed version, distributed in parallel with the original, takes the track's existing patience and stretches it further; in this case, the slowing is less of a structural reinvention than a doubling-down on what the original was already doing. Both versions sit on the genre's reliable playlists; both work as openers.

If Destruction Age is the scene's canonical-tier collaboration at its most fully realized, starly is LONOWN demonstrating what the project sounds like with the witch-house collaborator quieted — which is to say, softer, more melodic, and closer to the broader angelcore-as-genre center than to the akiaura-specific noisier edges.
