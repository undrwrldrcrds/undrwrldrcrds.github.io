What Is Angelcore? A Field Guide to Dance Music's Most Lacrimose Microgenre

It's not hyperpop. It's not dreampop. It's not whatever Spotify's algorithm thinks it is. A working definition of the sound pioneered by LONOWN, akiaura, and the ARCADIA / IMMINENT collective — and where the scene is moving now.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Field Notes

The first time you hear an angelcore track, the impulse is to file it somewhere familiar. It sounds, vaguely, like a slowed-down rave song that got left in the rain. There are hi-hats but they sound like glass. There's a vocal but it's been pitched into something that doesn't belong to a body anymore. There's a kick drum but it's been wrapped in so much reverb it functions less like a beat and more like the memory of one. The genre signposts are all there — house tempo, electronic instrumentation, dance-music architecture — and yet the song refuses to operate as dance music. It is, structurally, mourning something. Probably itself.

This essay is a working definition. Angelcore — like outsider house, hyperpop, or any other genre named retroactively by the internet — is not a fixed object. It is a set of aesthetic and structural tendencies that a small, mostly-online cohort of producers, listeners, and curators have started to recognize in each other's work. What follows is one attempt to draw the boundary.

The Pioneers: LONOWN, akiaura, and the ARCADIA / IMMINENT Collective

The lineage of angelcore as a coherent sound runs primarily through two producers — LONOWN and akiaura — and the orbiting collective ARCADIA and its label arm IMMINENT. akiaura, born December 1997 and based in Saint Petersburg, came up through the post-witch-house / corecore SoundCloud underground; the early discography leans toward the darker, more abrasive end of internet-native electronic music — closer to Crystal Castles than to anything you'd call house. The 2023 collaborative EP with LONOWN, Ketamine Girl, introduced both producers to a wider audience, and the EP's centerpiece Sleepwalker — across its original and the multiple slowed and remixed versions distributed in parallel — accumulated something on the order of 288 million Spotify streams, 800,000+ Shazam tags, and 600 million+ TikTok video uses, putting an unusual amount of commercial weight behind producers who otherwise operate in the most niche corner of the SoundCloud underground.

The genre's actual consolidation, however, dates to the 2024 LONOWN / akiaura collaborative album Destruction Age, followed by the 2025 collaborative album Fake Everything. Destruction Age is the record where the elements that would later be retroactively labeled "angelcore" first appear together as a coherent aesthetic project: house-tempo dance music drained of its functional dancefloor purpose, pitched and dissolved vocals, a late-2000s internet-era nostalgia welded to witch-house-derived noise and texture. The album reads now, two years later, as the genre's foundational text. Fake Everything is the project at full confidence, with the genre's vocabulary established and the producers ranging freely across it. Most of the producers currently working in the angelcore mode are in conversation with these records whether they say so or not.

LONOWN's solo trajectory is worth noting separately: by 2025, the project had crossed 6.7 million monthly Spotify listeners, placing it well above any reasonable definition of "underground" and into the territory of producers who can sustain a full-time career on streaming revenue alone. Most of those listeners arrive through algorithmic placement on broader phonk and electronic playlists rather than through the angelcore-specific curatorial apparatus — both akiaura and LONOWN came up in the wave and phonk scenes before the angelcore pivot, and the catalog still pulls listeners from those adjacent ecosystems — but the commercial scale matters for the genre's medium-term trajectory in ways that are worth watching.

ARCADIA and IMMINENT — the affiliated playlist / collective / label apparatus that has anchored the scene's editorial presence — function as the genre's institutional infrastructure. The ARCADIA-curated playlists ("ANGELCORE PLAYLIST," various themed mixtapes) are where the canon is being assembled in real time. If Destruction Age is angelcore's founding document, the ARCADIA / IMMINENT playlist run is its working canon — the place where producers find each other, where the boundary of the genre is being drawn week by week, and where a new artist gets read into the scene by being placed next to LONOWN and akiaura on a tracklist.

The Sound, Roughly

Angelcore tracks share a small handful of audible fingerprints. None of them are mandatory, but a track usually needs at least three to feel like it belongs to the scene:

  • Pitched, gauzy vocals. Frequently female-coded or androgynous, almost always processed past the point of legibility. The lyric is often a fragment — a half-sentence, a name, a refusal — looped until it becomes a texture rather than a statement.
  • House tempo, but emotionally horizontal. Tracks tend to sit in the 120–135 BPM range, the structural skeleton of house, but the kick is rarely the loudest element. The track moves but it doesn't quite drive.
  • Soft-attack synthesis with witch-house residue. Pads and supersaws dominate the upper register; the low end frequently carries the noise and grit inherited from akiaura's witch-house background. Anything percussive has been smeared with reverb or sidechain compression until it sounds like it's happening in the next room.
  • Atmospheric overload. Field recordings, vinyl crackle, wind, breath, distant voices, the static of a tape running out. The space around the music is doing as much work as the music itself.
  • Brevity. Most angelcore tracks live somewhere between 90 seconds and three minutes. The genre has internalized the attention economy of TikTok in a way older dance music hasn't — a direct downstream effect of Sleepwalker's trajectory.

Put differently: angelcore takes the surface of dance music — its tempo, its sonic palette, its emotional vocabulary of release and ascension — and refuses the structural payoff. The drop never arrives. The chorus never lands. The song dissolves before it can resolve. The result feels less like a dance track and more like the ten minutes after the dance, when the room has emptied and someone is still standing by the speaker, unwilling to leave.

Where the Name Comes From

"Angelcore" as a visual aesthetic predates the music by several years. The term floated around Tumblr and Pinterest in the late 2010s as shorthand for a soft, religious-iconography-adjacent visual mood — pale pinks and bone whites, ascendant photography, baroque cherubs scanned from old prayer books, the general affect of being one degree removed from the divine and trying to get back. The music, when it started consolidating around the LONOWN / akiaura collaborative axis in 2023–2024, borrowed both the name and the affect.

This is important. Angelcore is one of the rare recent microgenres where the visual and the sonic arrived nearly together, rather than the music being named after the fact. A track gets posted with the right kind of cover art — soft-focus photography, a serif font, a halo, the suggestion of dissolution — and listeners recognize it on sight before the first second has played. The aesthetic is doing curatorial work that the algorithm can't.

Adjacent Microgenres: Aero, Ærocore, Opiumcore, Outsider House

Angelcore is one node in a small cluster of overlapping micro-scenes. Worth knowing:

aero / ærocore

Aero — pioneered by yaego, skrta, and wzrdd — is angelcore's more uptempo, recombinant sibling. Where angelcore tends to dissolve, aero retains structural integrity: the beat is more present, the form is closer to actual dance music, and the source material draws heavily on early-mid 2010s EDM, y2k pop nostalgia, and trance lineage. yaego's 2025 EP ÆRO is the most fully realized statement of the sound. A separate primer on aero goes into the lineage in more detail.

opiumcore

Opiumcore is the broader umbrella the scene mostly sits under — borrowed from the Opium Records / Playboi Carti–adjacent visual world but extended to cover a wider electronic palette. Many angelcore artists describe their work as opiumcore to non-scene audiences; the term is more porous and less genre-specific.

outsider house

Outsider house — coined in the early 2010s to describe a wave of lo-fi, structurally unorthodox house music — is the closest sonic ancestor outside the LONOWN / akiaura lineage. Both share an interest in dance music's surface texture without much interest in the dancefloor's social function. A separate primer on outsider house covers the genealogy.

How It Lives Online

Angelcore is a Spotify-first, TikTok-second scene. This is not incidental. The genre's brevity, its reliance on cover art, and its emotional immediacy all map cleanly onto a streaming platform's incentive structure: songs that work in a 30-second preview, that read at thumbnail size, that resolve emotionally within a single TikTok-length window. The ARCADIA / IMMINENT playlists — alongside satellite curations from DJ Anemia, crier, prodvnxious, and others — function as both discovery engine and canon.

TikTok is where the scene's biggest commercial moments happen. Sleepwalker's 600M+ video uses is the obvious example, but a handful of LONOWN tracks, the angelrot single i'm falling, and various ARCADIA-aligned releases have crossed over into broader use as sound-on backgrounds for the kinds of melancholic, soft-focus videos that share the aesthetic family. This has produced an unusual situation where the genre's biggest commercial moments are happening on a platform most of the producers don't post on themselves.

A Wider Roll Call

Beyond the LONOWN / akiaura working partnership, the scene is still small enough that a list of its working producers is feasible without being totalizing. Among the names consistently associated with angelcore and its adjacent strands: Baby Jane, DJ Anemia, crier, DJ Pointless, deadwinter, riserayss, VNXIOUS, angeltears, ProdChxn, Crisys, fadevɇil, Oyabe, Krembuur, fervnt, and angelrot — most of whom appear directly on ARCADIA / IMMINENT releases, and other releases by Azurite Records, EPSILE, NCS, and other labels that have released witch house and angelcore. The cohort is geographically scattered (Saint Petersburg, Bay Area, the broader European underground) but stylistically coherent in the way that internet-native scenes tend to be.

Where to Start

If you've never heard an angelcore track, the quickest induction is the LONOWN and akiaura collaborative album Destruction Age (2024), which functions as the closest thing the scene has to a canonical document. From there: the ARCADIA / IMMINENT playlists on Spotify, the DJ Anemia and crier mixes that have circulated on YouTube, and the akiaura / LONOWN collaborative EP Ketamine Girl (2023) — particularly the original Sleepwalker, which despite its commercial trajectory remains a useful template for what the genre is structurally trying to do.

For a contemporary, single-artist entry point that demonstrates the genre's current state rather than its origins, angelrot's Soft Collapse (2025) is a representative album-length statement, and the newer single iconic (2026) is a more compact induction.

So Is Angelcore Real, or Is It a Marketing Term?

Both, of course. Every genre is a marketing term that some number of people agreed to take seriously enough to use. The question is whether the term is useful — whether it identifies something a listener can recognize, whether it lets producers find each other, whether it gives a critic a way to talk about a record without resorting to "the kind of thing where—"

By those standards, angelcore is real. It has a sound, a visual identity, a small canon of producers anchored by a documented foundational record, a curatorial apparatus in ARCADIA / IMMINENT, and enough internal coherence that a track can be misfiled and someone will notice. That's about as much as any genre has ever had at this stage. The rest is just waiting to see what it becomes.

The most likely outcome, judging by the precedent of every other internet-born microgenre of the last fifteen years, is that the term itself will be quietly abandoned by the producers who built the scene around the time it becomes legible to people outside it. The sound will persist; the name will start to feel embarrassing. This is fine. The work is the point. The name was only ever a way of finding each other in the dark.
