What Is Aero? Yaego, skrta, wzrdd and the Y2K-Recombinant Sound

A primer on aero — the underground dance sound pioneered by yaego, skrta, and wzrdd that grafts y2k club nostalgia onto 2010s EDM, nostalgic trance, and the residual DNA of swag house.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Field Notes

Aero is what happens when a producer who grew up watching 5 Gum commercials and ripping YouTube audio from 2008 Justin Timberlake performances sits down with FL Studio and refuses to pick a decade. The genre — pioneered by yaego, skrta, and wzrdd, formalized as a project on yaego's 2025 EP ÆRO — sits at an intersection that didn't really exist as a coherent aesthetic before it did: early-to-mid 2010s EDM bones, y2k pop and club nostalgia, the wide pads and emotional climbs of late-1990s trance, and the residual DNA of the swag house era. A TikTok-native sense of compression and immediacy ties the whole thing together.

If you want a one-line description: aero sounds like the soundtrack to an underground club you've never been to, but one you somehow remember. Slow-motion strobe lights. Sweaty bodies writhing under pulsing white light. The rave scene from Blade (1998), but with digital camera selfies and flip phones. Hypnotic, sinister, recombinant. Cool as hell. Someone on yaego's SoundCloud once commented on ill watch you burn us that they had "converted an inter-dimensional angelic being into an mp3 file" — and that line, more or less, is the working summary of what the genre does at its best.

If angelcore is dance music as mourning, aero is dance music as déjà vu — a track that feels like you've heard it before, in a place you've never been.

The Pioneers: yaego, skrta, wzrdd

The genre's center of gravity is yaego, whose ÆRO EP (2025) is the closest thing aero has to a foundational document. The 14-minute, 5-track release does a lot in not much time — lead single HARDX samples Justin Timberlake's Rock Your Body, but echoed and warped until it just blends into the synths and shaking bass. The opener musica electronica drops a dembow beat and an LMFAO "shake that" sample before ending abruptly in explosions, no cooldown. aerotheworld evokes Poker Face-era Gaga and then dives head-first into a disorienting matrix of synth notes. ill watch you burn us spends half its runtime simmering in perverse house tones, layering hi hats and pressurized atmosphere, then explodes. skin2skin has flickering chimes against sinister vibes — feels more like a beat sketch than a finished song, only 2 minutes long, but the beat is good.

yaego's broader catalog — singles EUROPA, The Sensation, TKOHHH, the Yaego & KUČKA collaboration Ketamina, the album 87.6 FM where EUROPA lives — establishes the working palette: chrome-plated y2k references, slowed-down samples, hypnotic synths, basslines that shake more than they drive. Visual identity is meticulously controlled: slow-motion strobes, overexposed and undersaturated photography, the visual language of a 2003 sci-fi movie put through three generations of YouTube compression. Grainy cover art with early-2000s club outfits on sweaty bodies, faces pixelated into anonymity. The aesthetic isn't decoration — it's a visualization of the sound itself.

wzrdd is the most frequent yaego collaborator and arguably the genre's co-author. The Yaego & wzrdd track aerotheworld — the genre's namesake document — and the Yaego & wzrdd collaboration ill watch you burn us are both core texts; wzrdd's solo work runs the same recombinant playbook but with a slightly harder rhythmic core. skrta sits closer to the trance-derived edge of the project — the Yaego & skrta track iseeghosts! [waitaminute] is one of the more rhythmically intact cuts in the EP cycle and one of the clearest demonstrations of how trance lineage gets metabolized into aero.

Three producers, enough overlapping releases, enough cross-collaboration, a tight enough aesthetic that listeners can recognize a fourth artist as part of the same scene without anyone having to spell it out. That's basically what a genre needs at the start.

The Working Definition

Aero tracks share a recognizable set of features. Not all are present on every record, but a track that hits most will pass as aero:

  • Y2K source material, contemporary execution. Samples and reference points from 2000–2008 pop and club music — slowed, chopped, recontextualized inside a contemporary production framework. The Justin Timberlake Rock Your Body echo running through HARDX is the textbook case. The LMFAO "shake that" drop in musica electronica is another.
  • Trance-derived emotional architecture. Late-1990s trance's slow build-and-release dynamic shows up across the aero catalog, often compressed into a much shorter format. The supersaw lead, the rising pad swell, the implied four-bar climb — present, but telescoped.
  • Mid-2010s EDM rhythmic bones. The percussion programming and mix balance owe more to 2014-era EDM than to either trance or house proper. When the hypnotic section gives way, it's to an aggressive beat, not a withdrawal.
  • Swag house residue. Particularly in the more uptempo and rap-adjacent corners of the catalog, the residual influence of late-2000s / early-2010s swag house (DJ Funk's Dance Mania lineage filtered through internet rap) shows up in the bass programming and in the way vocals get treated as percussion.
  • An overexposed-and-undersaturated visual aesthetic. The visual identity is doing as much work as the sound itself. Digital cameras, sweaty raves, slow-motion strobes, abstract futurism placed in y2k iconography — aero tracks tend to arrive with cover art doing curatorial work before the first note even plays.
  • Recession-era club music in a recession. One of the genre's quieter resonances: the y2k club music aero references was, in its original moment, the sound of a precarious economic era. The genre arrives, decades later, into another one — and the resonance is not accidental. Aero soundtracks driving through the city at night, fighting vampires in a rave, or whatever the modern equivalent ends up being.

The result is a sound that's simultaneously nostalgic and dystopian — a track that could plausibly play in either a 2003 club scene from a Wesley Snipes movie, or a 2030 underground rave attended exclusively by people who weren't alive in 2003.

The Tensions

Aero isn't fully formed yet, and that shows. The ÆRO EP runs 14 minutes across 5 tracks, and some of those tracks end abruptly — HARDX's intoxicating opening only lasts 90 seconds, musica electronica doesn't get a cooldown, skin2skin reads more as a 2-minute beat sketch than a full song. The hypnotic atmospheres these tracks build need more time to actually sink in. It's more than just dance music; it's an experience, and experiences need runtime. The genre's biggest open question right now is whether the founding producers extend their canonical work into longer-form material that lets these vibes resonate, or whether the short-format compression remains the working mode.

Worth saying directly: the abrupt endings, more than anything else, take you out of the immersion the rest of the music works so hard to build. That's worth fixing — and the question of whether subsequent releases address it is one of the more interesting things to watch about the genre's medium-term trajectory.

Where Aero Sits in the Wider Underground

Aero shares producers, listeners, and editorial real estate with angelcore. The two genres are not the same — aero retains structural integrity and dancefloor functionality in a way angelcore frequently refuses — but they live in the same playlists and have overlapping fanbases. The ærocore variant, sometimes stylized with the æ-ligature, is essentially the term used when a track sits closer to the angelcore center of the spectrum: softer, more dissolved, less driven. Both terms are in active use; neither has been definitively settled.

The relationship to outsider house is more distant but real. Both genres share an interest in foregrounding texture and atmosphere; both treat dance music as raw material to be recombined rather than as a fixed form to be perfected. The difference is largely temperament. Outsider house is patient, hardware-driven, and structurally austere. Aero is impatient, recombinant, and structurally maximalist within a short runtime.

Where to Start

  • yaego — ÆRO (2025). The foundational EP. HARDX and skin2skin are the clearest single-track demonstrations of what the genre is doing.
  • Yaego & wzrdd — aerotheworld, ill watch you burn us. The pair's collaborations are the genre's connective tissue; aerotheworld functions as a near-title-track for the whole project.
  • Yaego & skrta — iseeghosts! [waitaminute]. The trance-derived end of the spectrum.
  • yaego — EUROPA, The Sensation, TKOHHH. The solo singles map the genre's range.

Beyond the founding triad, the scene is still small enough that working outward through Spotify's "Fans also like" graph and the ARCADIA / IMMINENT-adjacent playlist apparatus will surface most of the active producers within an hour of listening.
