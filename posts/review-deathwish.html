akiaura, LONOWN & Baby Jane's "Deathwish" Is Angelcore at Its Most Legible

A review of the akiaura / LONOWN / Baby Jane collaboration — and a demonstration of what the genre sounds like when the vocal performance is allowed to stay forward.

by undrwrld_rcrds∙Review

Deathwish is the track in the scene's canonical-tier catalog that most cleanly demonstrates what the producers' working method does with a legible vocal performance on top. Where most of the duo's collaborative output processes vocals into pitched textural elements — the Sleepwalker sample, the various dissolved vocal fragments threaded through Destruction Age — Deathwish places Baby Jane's vocal forward in the mix and lets it carry the track's emotional argument.

This matters. Most contemporary angelcore tracks operate at a deliberate remove from the singer-songwriter convention; the genre's whole proposition is that vocals function as texture rather than as message. Deathwish partially abandons this — the Baby Jane performance is legible as a vocal performance, the lyric is recoverable, the emotional content reads on first listen rather than emerging across repeated plays. The track is, in this sense, the genre's most accessible single-track entry point. A listener arriving cold from a pop or alt-pop context can find purchase on Deathwish in a way that a track like Sleepwalker doesn't quite allow.

Around the vocal, the production runs the scene's canonical-tier house style at full conviction. The witch-house-derived low end is present but restrained; the supersaw pads are unusually warm; the percussion programming is more rhythmically intact than most of the duo's work, with a kick that drives the track forward instead of receding into atmosphere. The arrangement is, in effect, a demonstration that the angelcore production approach can support a more conventionally pop-coded song structure if the producers want it to. The fact that they mostly choose not to is a choice, not a constraint.

The track is the case for angelcore as a genre that can scale into broader audiences without losing its core moves.

Deathwish has, accordingly, become one of the tracks the genre's curators reach for when they want to demonstrate the scene to a listener who hasn't been inside it. The track has appeared on multiple ANGELCORE PLAYLIST volumes and across the broader IMMINENT-aligned editorial apparatus; it has also functioned as one of the genre's TikTok gateway records, in the same way Sleepwalker did but with the legibility advantage that comes from a forward vocal performance.

Baby Jane's broader catalog operates in the same vocally-forward register; if Deathwish lands, the producer's solo work is a reasonable next stop. The akiaura / LONOWN production scaffolding is what makes the track land at the scale it does, but the Baby Jane performance is what makes it stay.
